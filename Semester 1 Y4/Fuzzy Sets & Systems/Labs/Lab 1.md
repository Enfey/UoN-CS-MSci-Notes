- Did not use gaussian distribution to denote any part of the semantics of the terms, even though you can tune them according to representative data and naturally map onto mean and standard deviation which we have a global study and figures for
- The reason is that the membership degree for say, the model input for your term that has maximal height, will never become 0
- This is because when designing with the guidelines of overlap of adjacent terms, the tail of the distribution extends across the graph.
- Genetic outliers like Yao Ming would have a tiny membership degree for the term average, despite semantically as we understand, not being average whatsoever.
	- This hurts the explainability and says something we do not truly mean, which is key in the design of fuzzy logic systems
- Sex is largest single factor by which to **cleanly distinguish** height; the baseline individuals for comparison are from geographically dispersed regions, and have different ethnicities, both of which affect height.
- Giving terms semantics for all possible ways we could distinguish differing height would mean that we run out of reliable data quickly, whilst also having to ask for much more additional data such as continent, country, age etc, depending on how fine-grained we would want the classifications to be. 
- Use of a trapezoid for both tall and short made sense to me as there is a range of values that is generally considered to be definitely belonging to these terms, and not to average whatsoever. Beyond this bc region we taper down and cross with the average neighbouring term at 0.5 degree of membership, respectively. 
- The choice of a triangular membership function, and not a trapezoid is that the average is typically reported as a single, discrete value, so i feel it made sense to design the peak/normality around said value depending on population group/pooling and then taper down on both sides, rather than introduce a flat top which would be harder to justify.
